## **Welcome**

*(Page 2\)*

Somewhere along the way, you became roommates.

You still love each other. You still sleep in the same bed, split the bills, and show up for the same life. But the conversations got shorter. The silences got longer. And at some point you started wondering, quietly, whether you'd find your way back to each other — or whether this is just what marriage becomes.

Here's what we want you to know before you turn the page: you didn't fall out of love. You drifted. And drift has a cause you can actually work with.

Most couples don't lose each other because they stopped caring. They lose each other because they stopped practicing the small things that kept them close — noticing each other, speaking honestly, following through on small promises. The gap isn't love lost. **It's practice lost.** That's a much smaller problem to solve than it feels like from inside it.

**What the next 30 days actually are**

This is a structured, daily reset — not a crisis intervention, and not a program for recovering from an affair. If that's what you're facing, please see a licensed counselor first; this workbook isn't built for that kind of weight, and you deserve support that is. It's for the far more common ache of two people who love each other and have simply drifted into distance.

And if you're already seeing a counselor — good. This isn't a replacement for that work. Think of it as something you do alongside it, or as the first small step before it, not instead of it.

Each day takes about ten minutes. Most weeks include two short sessions to do together, with everything else designed for solo reflection — so if your spouse isn't ready to open a workbook yet, you can still start tonight, alone, and it will still work.

**A word on faith**

Scripture and prayer show up here as an invitation, never a requirement. If that's part of your life, you'll find it woven in gently. If it's not your season for that, skip those lines entirely — the practical work stands on its own.

**One more thing, plainly**

This workbook is educational, not therapy or medical advice. If abuse, addiction, or serious thoughts of separation are part of your story, please reach out to a licensed counselor. If you are ever in danger, contact local emergency services first — everything else can wait.

## **How This Reset Works**

*(Page 3\)*

**The rhythm**

Four weeks, one focus each:

* **Week 1 — Notice.** See where the distance actually shows up.  
* **Week 2 — Speak.** Reopen the conversation, safely.  
* **Week 3 — Trust.** Rebuild it in small, repeatable actions.  
* **Week 4 — Reconnect.** Choose each other again, out loud.

Most days are a short solo reflection — ten minutes, no homework mountain. A couple of days each week are marked **Couple**, meant to be done together. Everything else is yours alone to think through, even if your spouse never opens this book.

**If you're starting this alone**

You'll see a short note like this one whenever a day assumes two people. Skip the "together" instructions, answer the reflection as it applies to you, and keep going. Real change from one partner's effort alone is slower, but it's real — and the Communication Repair Script in Week 2 is written specifically to help you reopen things gently, on your own timeline.

**What's inside**

Alongside the daily pages, you'll use four tools as you go:

* The **Communication Repair Script** (page 14\) — exact words for hard conversations.  
* The **Trust Micro-Habits Menu** (page 22\) — small daily actions, not grand gestures.  
* The **Day 30 Recommitment Ceremony** (page 30\) — a real moment of choosing each other again.  
* The **Weekly Check-In Card**, which you'll use tonight, and again on Days 7, 14, and 21\.

**Tonight — before Day 1**

Sit down together for five minutes (or alone, if you're starting solo) and answer three questions honestly:

1. How are we, really?  
2. What do we need right now?  
3. What's one small next step?

Write your answers somewhere you'll find them again — the back of this page, a notes app, anywhere. You'll compare them to how things feel by Day 30\. That's it. That's tonight's whole task, and it's already a small, real start.

## **Week 1 · Notice**

*(Pages 4–10)*

You can't reset what you can't see. This week isn't about fixing anything yet — it's about noticing, clearly and without judgment, where the distance actually shows up and what you're quietly longing for underneath it. Nothing here requires your spouse's participation to be true or useful.

### **Day 1 — Name the Distance**

*Page 4 · Couple (or Solo) · Quick Win*

If you did the Check-In Card last night, you've already started. If not, do it now — it takes five minutes and it's the whole reason today counts as a win.

**Why this matters:** naming a feeling out loud, even just to yourselves on paper, changes it. "We're drifting" stops being a vague dread and starts being something you can actually work on, one Tuesday at a time.

**Today's reflection:**

* Read back what you wrote on the Check-In Card. Does anything surprise you?  
* Without blaming anyone, finish this sentence: "Lately, our marriage has felt more like \_\_\_\_\_\_ than a partnership."

**If you're starting this alone:** answer both questions for yourself. There's no wrong version of this — noticing is never something you can get wrong.

*If it helps: a short prayer of thanks for even this small first step is a good way to close today.*

### **Day 2 — Where the Roommates Began**

*Page 5 · Solo*

Roommate distance rarely arrives all at once. It builds from a hundred small trades — a real conversation swapped for a logistics update, again and again, until logistics is most of what's left.

**Why this matters:** you can't undo years of drift in a day, but you *can* start noticing the exact moment connection gets swapped for scheduling. That's the moment worth protecting next time.

**Today's reflection:**

* Think of one conversation from this past week. Was it about connection, or was it about logistics — schedules, bills, who's picking up the kids?  
* If it was logistics, what might a two-minute version of that same conversation have looked like with one extra, personal sentence added?

**If you're starting this alone:** you're only tracking your own side of these conversations today. That's enough data to work with.

---

### **Day 3 — The Bid You Almost Missed**

*Page 6 · Solo*

Researchers who've spent decades watching how couples actually interact — not what they say they do, but what they do in the moment — found something surprisingly small mattered most: how partners respond to tiny, everyday bids for attention. A sigh. A comment about the weather. A "hey, look at this." The couples who stayed close weren't the ones with no problems. They were the ones who noticed these small bids and turned toward them, again and again.

**Why this matters:** you don't need a grand romantic gesture this week. You need to catch one small bid you'd otherwise miss.

**Today's reflection:**

* Recall a moment recently when your spouse said something small — a comment, a question, a sigh — and you were distracted, tired, or elsewhere.  
* Without guilt, just name it: "I think I missed a bid when \_\_\_\_\_\_."

**If you're starting this alone:** this is about your own noticing, not your spouse's behavior. You're building the muscle, not scoring anyone.

### **Day 4 — What Am I Actually Longing For?**

*Page 7 · Solo*

Under almost every complaint about a marriage is a softer, more vulnerable longing that rarely gets said out loud. "You never listen" is often really "I want to feel important to you." "We never do anything" is often really "I miss feeling chosen."

**Why this matters:** you can't ask for what you haven't named, even to yourself.

**Today's reflection:**

* Set the complaints aside for a moment. Underneath the frustration, what are you actually longing for — closeness? Being known? Feeling chosen? Partnership?  
* Write it in one honest sentence: "What I'm really longing for is \_\_\_\_\_\_."

**If you're starting this alone:** keep this one private for now if you need to. You'll have a safe, structured way to share it in Week 2\.

---

### **Day 5 — The Story I've Been Telling Myself**

*Page 8 · Solo*

Every drifting marriage comes with a private narrative running underneath it — "we're just not compatible anymore," "this is just what marriage becomes," "it's too late to fix this." These stories feel like facts. Usually, they're just the loudest interpretation available at 11 p.m. on a hard day.

**Why this matters:** the story you tell yourself about your marriage shapes what you're willing to try. A slightly gentler, equally true story opens up more options than a hopeless one does.

**Today's reflection:**

* Write the private story you've been telling yourself about your marriage — one or two sentences, no editing.  
* Now write one alternative story that's just as true but gentler. (Example: "We're just not compatible" → "We used to know how to reach each other, and we can learn that again.")

**If you're starting this alone:** this exercise works the same either way — you're examining your own story, not predicting your spouse's.

### **Day 6 — A Small Turn Toward**

*Page 9 · Couple*

Today is about doing something with everything you've noticed this week — one small, concrete turn toward each other.

**Why this matters:** you don't need a resolved conversation to start turning toward each other again. You need one small, repeatable moment of attention.

**Today's session (10 minutes together):**

* Each of you name one specific bid you'll try to notice and respond to this week — a tone of voice, a certain sigh, a particular kind of question.  
* Agree on a simple signal for "I need you to turn toward me right now" that either of you can use, even mid-argument.

**If you're going through this alone:** choose your own bid to watch for in your spouse this week, without announcing it. You're practicing the noticing either way — sharing the agreement is a bonus, not a requirement.

### **Day 7 — What Notice Week Showed Us**

*Page 10 · Couple · Week 1 Integration*

One week of noticing is behind you. Today is for taking stock — gently, without turning it into a performance review.

**Why this matters:** naming what you noticed, out loud, to each other, is itself a small act of reconnection — often the first real one in a while.

**Today's session (10–15 minutes together):**

* Each share one thing you noticed this week about the distance, and one thing you noticed you were longing for. Listen without fixing or defending.  
* Complete the Weekly Check-In Card again. Compare it, gently, to Day 1's answers — not to judge progress, just to see it.

**If you're going through this alone:** write both answers for yourself, and consider sharing just one of them with your spouse this week, low-pressure, whenever it feels natural. That single shared sentence can be enough to open the door.

*If it helps: close the week with a short prayer of thanks for what you're both willing to see, even in its early, unfinished shape.*

## **Week 2 · Speak**

*(Pages 11–17)*

"Just communicate more" is true and useless at the same time — you already know you should talk. What you're missing isn't motivation. It's the actual words for the moment things get tense. This week hands you those words, then gives you room to practice using them before it matters. Every tool this week — including the script on page 14 — works whether or not your spouse is reading along.

### **Day 8 — Why Words Got Hard**

*Page 11 · Solo*

Most couples don't stop talking because they ran out of things to say. They stop because talking started to feel risky — like it might turn into a fight, or into silence that's somehow worse than a fight.

**Why this matters:** under stress, most people default to one of two patterns — pushing to resolve things right now, or going quiet until it blows over. Neither is wrong. Both make sense as protection. But naming your own pattern is the first step to choosing something else when it counts.

**Today's reflection:**

* Under stress, do you tend to push to talk it out immediately, or go quiet and pull back? Neither answer is a character flaw — just notice which is yours.  
* What does your spouse tend to do? (If you're not sure, that's useful information too.)

**If you're going through this alone:** you can only be sure of your own pattern today, and that's exactly enough to work with this week.

---

### **Day 9 — Reacting vs. Repairing**

*Page 12 · Solo*

Every couple argues. What separates the ones who stay close from the ones who drift apart isn't the absence of conflict — it's what happens in the middle of it. A repair attempt is any small thing, said or done, that puts the brakes on before things spiral: a joke, an apology, a hand on the arm, a simple "can we slow down."

**Why this matters:** you've probably already made repair attempts without having a name for them. Recognizing them makes you more likely to use them on purpose.

**Today's reflection:**

* Think of a recent disagreement. Was there a moment either of you tried to de-escalate — a joke, an apology, a pause? What happened when they did?  
* If no repair attempt was made, what might one have sounded like?

**If you're going through this alone:** you're building awareness you'll use starting tomorrow, whether or not your spouse is doing this exercise alongside you.

---

### **Day 10 — Naming What's Underneath**

*Page 13 · Solo*

Most complaints are a smaller, sharper version of a bigger, softer feeling. "You're always on your phone" is often really "I feel invisible when we're in the same room." The complaint gets said. The feeling underneath rarely does.

**Why this matters:** the sharp version tends to start a fight. The soft version tends to start a real conversation.

**Today's reflection:**

* Write down one complaint you've had recently — the sharp, surface version, exactly as it would come out in the moment.  
* Now rewrite it, naming the softer feeling underneath: "When \_\_\_\_\_\_ happens, I feel \_\_\_\_\_\_."

**If you're going through this alone:** keep both versions somewhere you can find them. Tomorrow's tool is where you'll learn when and how to actually say the second one.

### **Day 11 — The Communication Repair Script**

*Page 14 · Solo (tool) · Fully usable now*

You don't need to be naturally good at hard conversations. You need six lines, ready before you need them — the same way you'd keep a fire extinguisher somewhere you can reach it without thinking, not because you plan to need it, but because when you do, thinking clearly is the first thing to go.

**How to use this:** say the line out loud, exactly as written, the moment you notice things heating up — or right before a conversation you've been avoiding. The formality is the point. A scripted line signals "I'm trying to de-escalate" more clearly than an improvised one, especially to a partner who's braced for the opposite.

**The six lines:**

1. **"Can we start over?"** — Use the moment you hear your own voice sharpen.  
2. **"I think I said that wrong — let me try again."** — Use right after you notice you were harsher than you meant to be.  
3. **"I'm not trying to win this. I'm trying to understand you."** — Use when the conversation starts feeling like a debate with a winner.  
4. **"Can you say more about what that felt like for you?"** — Use when your partner shares something vulnerable and your first instinct is to defend yourself instead.  
5. **"I need five minutes before I can talk about this calmly."** — Use when you feel yourself flooding. Name an actual time you'll return — "give me five minutes" — and then return at five minutes, not when it's convenient.  
6. **"You're more important to me than being right about this."** — Use when the topic is small but the tension feels bigger than the topic deserves.

**Two prompts that make honesty feel safer**, worth using before a hard conversation rather than during one:

* "What's one thing that would help you feel safe telling me the truth right now?"  
* "If I promise not to react right away, is there something you've been wanting to say?"

**If you're going through this alone:** every one of these six lines works even if your spouse has never seen this book. You can say "can we start over?" in the middle of a tense moment regardless of who else knows why you're saying it. The lines don't require your partner's participation to work — only your willingness to say them first.

**Today's reflection:** which of the six lines is hardest for you to imagine saying out loud? That's usually the one worth practicing first.

### **Day 12 — Practicing the Script**

*Page 15 · Couple*

A script only works if it's already familiar the first time you need it under pressure. Today is for trying one line on purpose, in a low-stakes moment, so it's not brand new the first time it actually matters.

**Today's session (10 minutes together):**

* Pick one small, real friction from the past week or so — nothing catastrophic, just something mildly annoying.  
* Practice bringing it up using one line from yesterday's script. Notice how it changes the shape of the conversation, even artificially.  
* Afterward, each share which line felt most natural to say, and which felt stiff or awkward.

**If you're going through this alone:** practice saying one line out loud to yourself, or use it for real the next time a small friction comes up — even without announcing that you're "doing an exercise." The line works the same either way.

### **Day 13 — When Honesty Feels Risky**

*Page 16 · Solo*

Somewhere in every drifting marriage is at least one honest thing that hasn't been said — not because it's shameful, but because saying it feels like it might change something, and change feels risky when you're already unsteady.

**Why this matters:** the things left unsaid don't disappear. They just sit underneath everything else, quietly making every other conversation a little more guarded.

**Today's reflection:**

* Is there something you've been holding back — not a confession, just an honest thought or feeling you haven't voiced? Name it for yourself, even if you're not ready to say it aloud yet.  
* What's the actual fear underneath not saying it? (Being misunderstood? Starting a fight? Being seen as needy or difficult?)

**If you're going through this alone:** you don't have to share this today, or even this month. Naming it privately is real progress on its own, and the script above will be here whenever you're ready to use it.

*If it helps: a short prayer for courage to be honest — not for the perfect words, just the willingness — is a quiet way to close today.*

## **Week 3 · Trust**

*(Pages 18–24)*

Trust doesn't come back because of one big gesture. It comes back the same way it left — in small moments, repeated until they're reflexive. This week isn't about grand romance. It's about picking one small thing and actually doing it, on purpose, more than once. Several of this week's rituals work as one-sided gifts, so a lone reader has just as clear a path through this week as a couple doing it together.

### **Day 15 — Trust Is Built in Seconds, Not Speeches**

*Page 18 · Solo*

If you ask most long-married couples what keeps them close, they rarely point to a grand vacation or a big speech. They point to small things they do without fail — a particular greeting, a nightly check-in, a habit so ordinary it barely looks like effort from the outside.

**Why this matters:** you're not looking for a dramatic fix this week. You're looking for one small, protected moment you can actually keep.

**Today's reflection:**

* Did you and your spouse used to have a small ritual — a greeting, a routine, a habit — that's quietly disappeared? What was it?  
* What made it easy to keep, back when you kept it?

**If you're going through this alone:** you can start a ritual on your own side of the relationship this week — greeting your spouse a certain way, asking a particular question — without needing their buy-in first. Small, one-sided consistency is still real trust-building.

*If it helps: a short prayer of thanks for the ordinary, unremarkable moments of a marriage is a good way to close today — they carry more weight than they get credit for.*

### **Day 16 — Pick One Ritual**

*Page 19 · Solo*

You'll get a full menu of options tomorrow-after-next. Today is just for narrowing your thinking, so you're not choosing on the spot later.

**Why this matters:** a ritual only works if it's small enough to actually keep. Ambitious plans get abandoned by week two. Tiny ones survive.

**Today's reflection:**

* Of these three starting points — a real greeting at reunion, a genuine "how was your day," a moment of gratitude before sleep — which feels most doable for you this week, not someday?  
* What's gotten in the way of a habit like this sticking before?

**If you're going through this alone:** choose a ritual you can start on your own end regardless of participation — a warmer greeting, a genuine question — and let it be a gift with no immediate return expected.

### **Day 17 — The Kept Promise**

*Page 20 · Solo*

Trust isn't really about big declarations. It's built — or worn down — in small promises: "I'll call you at lunch," "I'll take care of it," "I won't forget." Kept or missed, these add up faster than almost anything else in a marriage.

**Why this matters:** you don't need to fix every broken promise from the past. You need to start noticing the small ones you're keeping and missing right now.

**Today's reflection:**

* Think of one small promise — yours or your spouse's — that was kept this week. How did it land, even if it seemed minor?  
* Think of one that was missed. Try to notice this without building a case against anyone — just observe it.

**If you're going through this alone:** focus today on the promises you're keeping. That's the half of this you have full control over.

### **Day 18 — Trust as a Daily Deposit**

*Page 21 · Solo*

Think of trust like a shared account. Every small positive moment — a kind word, a kept promise, a moment of real attention — is a deposit. Every dismissal or broken promise is a withdrawal. Neither one is dramatic on its own. The balance is what matters over time.

**Why this matters:** you don't need one huge deposit to feel safer together. You need more small deposits than withdrawals, most weeks.

**Today's reflection:**

* What's one small deposit you made into your marriage this week — something easy to overlook, but real?  
* What's one small deposit your spouse made, that you might not have acknowledged out loud?

**If you're going through this alone:** notice your own deposits this week without needing them matched right away. Consistency, not immediate reciprocity, is what rebuilds trust.

*If it helps: name one thing you're grateful your spouse has deposited into this marriage over the years, even during the drift — and consider telling them, plainly, sometime this week.*

### **Day 19 — The Trust Micro-Habits Menu**

*Page 22 · Solo (tool) · Fully usable now*

Here's the menu. Pick one — not all of these, just one — and keep it for at least a week before you consider adding another. Small and kept beats ambitious and abandoned, every time.

**Choose one to start:**

* **The six-second kiss.** A real one, not a peck, at a transition point — leaving for the day or coming home.  
* **The welcome-home routine.** The first two minutes after reunion are phone-down, full attention, before anything else gets discussed.  
* **The daily map question.** Ask, "what's one thing you're looking forward to — or dreading — today?"  
* **The stress-reducing ten.** Ten minutes each day just listening to your partner's outside-world stress. No fixing, no advice — just listening.  
* **One spoken appreciation.** Name one specific thing your partner did, out loud, every day.  
* **The kept-promise note.** Tell your partner the moment you follow through on something small you said you would — "hey, I did that thing."  
* **Tech-free dinner.** Phones away for one meal a day, even if it's only fifteen minutes.  
* **Bedtime gratitude.** One sentence each, last thing before sleep: "today I was grateful for \_\_\_\_\_\_."

**If you're going through this alone:** several of these work as one-sided gifts — the spoken appreciation, the kept-promise note, a genuine welcome-home moment — regardless of whether your spouse is doing anything differently in return. Start there.

**Today's reflection:** circle the one you'll try first. Tell your spouse which one and why, if you're doing this together — or simply start it quietly this week if you're doing this alone.

**Why this matters:** expecting a finished, permanent fix sets you up to feel like a failure the first time an old pattern resurfaces. Expecting ongoing practice sets you up to keep going when that happens.

### **Day 30 — The Recommitment Ceremony**

*Page 30 · Couple · The finale*

This isn't legally binding, and it doesn't need an officiant — just the two of you, ten quiet minutes, and the letters you started on Day 24\.

**Before you begin:** find a moment without distraction — after the kids are asleep, on a porch, wherever feels right. This doesn't need to be elaborate. It needs to be real.

**Opening** *(read together, or one partner reads aloud):*

> "Thirty days ago, we named something true: we hadn't fallen out of love. We'd drifted. Today, we're choosing to stop drifting."

**Spoken commitments** *(each partner completes and reads their own aloud):*

* "In the next season, I choose to notice \_\_\_\_\_\_."  
* "In the next season, I choose to speak \_\_\_\_\_\_, even when it's hard."  
* "In the next season, I choose to protect our ritual of \_\_\_\_\_\_."  
* "I choose you again — not because these thirty days fixed everything, but because I want to keep choosing you."

**Optional faith blessing:** a short prayer of thanks for the last 30 days, and a request for continued grace in the marriage — in your own words, or a pastor's words if you have one you trust.

**Optional gesture:** exchange the letters you started on Day 24, light a candle, or simply hold hands in silence for one full minute.

**If you're going through this alone:** you can absolutely do a version of this by yourself — speak your own commitments aloud, even to an empty room, and keep the letter for when your spouse is ready to read it. A ceremony of one is still a real, spoken choice. It's not nothing. It might be the thing that changes everything else.

### **Beyond Day 30**

*Still page 30*

The reset ends here. The habit doesn't.

You don't need to keep doing all thirty days' worth of practices starting tomorrow. You need to choose one thing — the Check-In Card, one line from the script, one ritual from the menu — and keep doing that one thing into month two. The couples who hold onto what they've rebuilt aren't the ones who do everything. They're the ones who keep doing one small thing, on purpose, past the point where it feels new.

Choose your one thing now, while today is still fresh. Write it down. That's the last task of these 30 days, and maybe the most important one.

## **Bonus 1 — The Communication Repair Script (Printable)**

*One page. Print it, cut it down to a card if you want, and keep it somewhere you'll actually see it — the fridge, a wallet, the inside cover of this book.*

**Use these when a conversation is heating up, or right before one you've been avoiding. Say the line exactly as written — the formality is the point.**

1. **"Can we start over?"** — the moment your voice sharpens.  
2. **"I think I said that wrong — let me try again."** — right after you hear yourself land harder than you meant to.  
3. **"I'm not trying to win this. I'm trying to understand you."** — when it starts feeling like a debate.  
4. **"Can you say more about what that felt like for you?"** — when your instinct is to defend instead of listen.  
5. **"I need five minutes before I can talk about this calmly."** — name an actual time, then come back at it.  
6. **"You're more important to me than being right about this."** — when the topic is small but the tension isn't.

**Two lines to ask before a hard conversation, not during one:**

* "What's one thing that would help you feel safe telling me the truth right now?"  
* "If I promise not to react right away, is there something you've been wanting to say?"

**Make your own.** These six work because they're specific, short, and said out loud on purpose — not because they're the only options. If none of them sound like you, a repair attempt doesn't have to be a sentence at all:

* **Say something** — a phrase, like the six above.  
* **Do something physical** — a hand on the arm, leaning in instead of away, softening your face.  
* **Break the tension** — a shared joke that lightens the moment without dismissing the issue.  
* **Do something small and practical** — bring your partner water mid-conversation, sit down next to them instead of standing.

Pick whichever shape actually sounds like you, and write your own line or gesture for it. A repair attempt only works if you'll actually say or do it.

## **Bonus 2 — The Recommitment Letter Template**

*Start this on Day 24\. Exchange it on Day 30\. Write in your own words — the prompts are just a place to start, not a form to fill out perfectly.*

**What I've noticed about us this month** Not a list of problems — just what's actually different, or actually still true, that you've noticed over these 30 days.

> *Starter: "I noticed that when we \_\_\_\_\_\_, something shifted..."*

**What I appreciate in you that I don't say enough** Specific beats general. "You're a good person" says less than "you called your mom back even though you were exhausted, and I noticed."

> *Starter: "I don't say this enough, but I appreciate how you \_\_\_\_\_\_."*

**What I'm choosing, starting today** Not a resolution to be perfect — a specific, honest choice about how you want to show up.

> *Starter: "Starting today, I'm choosing to \_\_\_\_\_\_, even when it's hard."*

**What I promise for the next season** Smaller and more concrete beats grand and vague. "I promise to ask about your day and actually listen" is more usable than "I promise to be better."

> *Starter: "For the next season, I promise \_\_\_\_\_\_."*

**Before you exchange letters:** read your own back once, out loud, alone. If a line feels performative instead of true, rewrite it. This letter is for your marriage, not for effect.

## **Bonus 3 — 30 Nights of Prayer for Your Marriage**

*One short prayer a night, said together if you can, said alone if you're the only one praying tonight. Each week's prayers echo that week's theme. Scripture references are given so you can look up and pray in whatever translation you already trust — the exact wording is yours to choose.*

**Week 1 · Notice**

* **Night 1:** God, help us see clearly — not to assign blame, just to notice where the distance actually lives. *Reference: Psalm 139:23–24*  
* **Night 2:** Show us the moment connection quietly became logistics, and give us the patience to notice it without shame. *Reference: Proverbs 4:23*  
* **Night 3:** Slow us down enough to actually hear each other's small, easy-to-miss bids for attention. *Reference: James 1:19*  
* **Night 4:** We bring You what we're really longing for, even the parts we haven't said out loud to each other yet. *Reference: Psalm 62:8*  
* **Night 5:** Where we've told ourselves a hopeless story about this marriage, help us see the truer, gentler one. *Reference: Isaiah 43:18–19*  
* **Night 6:** Give us the courage for one small turn toward each other this week, however small it feels. *Reference: 1 Thessalonians 5:11*  
* **Night 7:** Thank You for whatever we were willing to notice this week, even the uncomfortable parts. *Reference: Psalm 100:4–5*

**Week 2 · Speak**

* **Night 8:** Where fear has kept us quiet, replace it with the steadiness to speak honestly. *Reference: 2 Timothy 1:7*  
* **Night 9:** Make us quick to repair and slow to escalate, even when we're tired or hurt. *Reference: Matthew 5:9*  
* **Night 10:** Help us find the softer truth underneath our sharpest complaints. *Reference: Proverbs 20:5*  
* **Night 11:** Let the words we practice this week actually land as gentle, even under pressure. *Reference: Proverbs 16:24*  
* **Night 12:** Season our speech with grace tonight, especially in the small, ordinary friction. *Reference: Colossians 4:6*  
* **Night 13:** Cast out whatever fear is keeping either of us from being fully honest. *Reference: 1 John 4:18*  
* **Night 14:** Thank You for whatever honest words got said this week — help us keep speaking truth in love. *Reference: Ephesians 4:15*

**Week 3 · Trust**

* **Night 15:** Remind us that we're stronger noticing and rebuilding this together than either of us could alone. *Reference: Ecclesiastes 4:9–10*  
* **Night 16:** Teach us to number these small, ordinary days rightly, instead of waiting for a dramatic one. *Reference: Psalm 90:12*  
* **Night 17:** Let our yes be yes this week — help us keep the small promises we make to each other. *Reference: Matthew 5:37*  
* **Night 18:** Thank You for the small deposits we made into each other this week, even the ones that went unnoticed. *Reference: 1 Thessalonians 5:18*  
* **Night 19:** Give us the patience to keep this small habit going, even after the novelty wears off. *Reference: Galatians 6:9*  
* **Night 20:** Help us be genuinely devoted to each other in this one small, ordinary ritual tonight. *Reference: Romans 12:10*  
* **Night 21:** We commit this rebuilding to You, trusting it more than our own uncertain feelings about how it's going. *Reference: Proverbs 3:5–6*

**Week 4 · Reconnect**

* **Night 22:** Thank You for whatever has actually shifted this month, however small it looks from the outside. *Reference: Psalm 126:3*  
* **Night 23:** Thank You that Your mercy toward us — and the mercy we owe each other — is new again tomorrow morning. *Reference: Lamentations 3:22–23*  
* **Night 24:** As we write to each other tonight, help our words be true, specific, and kind. *Reference: Song of Solomon 8:6*  
* **Night 25:** Be the third strand holding what the two of us alone couldn't hold as well. *Reference: Ecclesiastes 4:12*  
* **Night 26:** Whatever one word describes this month, thank You for the chance to keep choosing better words going forward. *Reference: Proverbs 18:21*  
* **Night 27:** Thank You for carrying the good work You started in this marriage this month, even in its unfinished shape. *Reference: Philippians 1:6*  
* **Night 28:** Help us forgive tonight — each other, and ourselves — the way we've been forgiven. *Reference: Colossians 3:13*  
* **Night 29:** Tomorrow we choose again. Tonight, prepare our hearts to mean it. *Reference: Joshua 24:15*  
* **Night 30:** Wherever this marriage goes next, we go together — thank You for thirty days that brought us back to that choice. *Reference: Ruth 1:16–17*

## **Bonus 4 — The Weekly Check-In Card**

*One page. Five minutes. Use it tonight before Day 1, again on Days 7, 14, and 21, and then monthly after Day 30 — this is the one habit built specifically to outlast the workbook.*

**Date: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_**

**1\. How are we, really?** Not a status update — an honest one-line read on the actual state of things right now.

---

**2\. What do we need right now?** Not a wish list — one real, current need, yours or the marriage's.

---

**3\. What's one small next step?** Not a plan for the month — one specific, doable thing before the next check-in.

---

**How to use this:**

* Five minutes, no longer. This isn't meant to become another hard conversation — if something bigger surfaces, name it and set a separate time to talk it through.  
* Answer honestly even when the honest answer is "not great." That's what the card is for.  
* If you're going through this alone, fill it out for yourself anyway. Your own honest read on "how are we, really?" is still useful information, with or without a second set of answers.  
* After Day 30, keep this going monthly. It's the smallest habit in this entire workbook, and often the one that matters most a year from now.
