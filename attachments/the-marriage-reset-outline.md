## 1. Research Grounding (verified, current sources)

Used for the *underlying* techniques. Where a piece is the creator's own framing rather than a citable finding, it's labeled "creator framing" — not dressed up as research.

**Week 1 — Notice**
- Gottman Institute's research on **bids for connection**: partners who stayed together turned toward each other's small bids ~86% of the time vs. ~33% for those who later divorced — the biggest single predictor found in that research. Source: Gottman Institute; *The Relationship Cure* / *The Love Prescription*.
- Sue Johnson's **Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)**: distress usually sits on a surface "protest" or "withdrawal" behavior, with a softer, more vulnerable longing underneath it. Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight* (2008); Christian-adapted edition *Created for Connection* (Johnson & Sanderfer).
- Noticing one's own narrative about the relationship — creator framing, not a clinical claim.

**Week 2 — Speak**
- Gottman's **repair attempts**: any statement or action that de-escalates conflict; Gottman calls formalized repair phrases a "secret weapon," and found that couples who use them recover from conflict far more reliably. Source: Gottman, *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*; Gottman Institute repair-attempt research.
- EFT's distinction between **secondary (protective) emotion and primary (vulnerable) emotion** as the deeper thing worth naming. Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight*.
- "Honesty feels safe when named as an invitation" — creator framing, paired with a faith-based lens on truth-telling (see Week 2, Day 13).

**Week 3 — Trust**
- Gottman's **Rituals of Connection**: small, repeated, protected moments (a six-second kiss, a "welcome home" routine, a daily stress-reducing conversation) that couples who stay close maintain deliberately. Source: Gottman Institute, "The Little Things That Keep Love Strong"; Gottman & Gottman, *Eight Dates*.
- The **emotional bank account** concept — small positive deposits building the reserve that carries a couple through hard moments. Source: Gottman Institute.

**Week 4 — Reconnect**
- EFT's final consolidation stage — couples naming their new pattern and choosing it going forward. Source: Johnson, *Hold Me Tight*; *Created for Connection* (Christian adaptation, co-authored with Kenneth Sanderfer, blends EFT with biblical principles — the closest citable bridge between the clinical and faith-based material you want).
- Gary Thomas's **Sacred Marriage** framing — marriage as a relationship that shapes character, not just happiness. Source: Thomas, *Sacred Marriage*; *Devotions for a Sacred Marriage*.
- Vow-renewal/recommitment ceremony conventions (informal, not legally binding, built from reflection + spoken commitment + optional ring or symbolic gesture) — standard officiant-resource convention, adapted here for a private at-home version. Creator framing for the specific wording.

**Not used:** no invented statistics, no unverifiable "studies show" claims. Anywhere a specific bible verse is referenced, only the reference (book/chapter/verse) is given — the exact translation and wording is your call when you write final copy, since translations are separately copyrighted.

## 4. Week 1 — Notice (Pages 4–10)

**Theme:** You can't reset what you can't see. This week is pure observation — where the distance actually shows up day to day, and what each of you is quietly longing for underneath it.

**Summary:** Most couples don't drift because they stopped loving each other; they drift because small bids for connection — a comment, a sigh, a question — started going unanswered, and neither partner noticed it happening. This week trains that noticing muscle before asking anyone to change anything.

**Technique:** Gottman's bids-for-connection research; EFT's idea that a surface complaint usually sits on top of a softer, unspoken longing.

**Takeaway:** By Day 7, each partner can name one specific moment they turned away from a bid this week — without blame, just awareness.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | Couple (Quick Win) | Name the Distance | Complete the Weekly Check-In Card together — first honest read of "how are we, really?" |
| 2 | 5 | Solo | Where the Roommates Began | Identify one recurring exchange this week that was logistics-only (schedules, bills, chores) |
| 3 | 6 | Solo | The Bid You Almost Missed | Recall a small bid for connection (comment, sigh, question) that went unanswered recently |

| 5 | 8 | Solo | The Story I've Been Telling Myself | Write the private narrative about the marriage, then write one gentler, equally true alternative |

## 5. Week 2 — Speak (Pages 11–17)

**Theme:** Drift thrives in silence and shrinks the moment you speak — carefully, with the right words instead of just "more communication."

**Summary:** "Just communicate more" is right advice and the wrong instruction. Couples who recover from conflict well aren't the ones who never fight — they're the ones who know a small, specific set of phrases that de-escalate a hard moment before it spirals. This week hands over exactly those words.

**Technique:** Gottman's repair-attempt research (formalized phrases as a "secret weapon"); EFT's distinction between the protective reaction and the vulnerable feeling underneath it.

**Takeaway:** By Day 14, both partners have tried at least one line from the Communication Repair Script in a real (even minor) moment of friction.

## 6.1 — Page 14: The Communication Repair Script (fully written)

*Use this when a conversation is heating up, or right before a conversation you've been avoiding. Say the line out loud — the formality is the point; it signals to your partner that you're trying to de-escalate, not attack.*

1. **"Can we start over?"** — Use the moment you hear your own voice sharpen.
2. **"I think I said that wrong — let me try again."** — Use right after you notice you were harsher than you meant to be.
3. **"I'm not trying to win this. I'm trying to understand you."** — Use when the conversation starts feeling like a debate.
4. **"Can you say more about what that felt like for you?"** — Use when your partner shares something vulnerable and you're tempted to defend instead of listen.
5. **"I need five minutes before I can talk about this calmly."** — Use when you feel yourself flooding; name a specific time you'll return to it, and actually return.
6. **"You're more important to me than being right about this."** — Use when the topic is small but the tension is big.

**Two prompts that make honesty feel safer before you use the script:**
- "What's one thing that would help you feel safe telling me the truth right now?"
- "If I promise not to react right away, is there something you've been wanting to say?"

## 6.2 — Page 22: The Trust Micro-Habits Menu (fully written)

*Pick one. Not all of these — one. Keep it for at least a week before adding another.*

- **The six-second kiss** — a real one, not a peck, at a transition point (leaving, coming home).
- **The welcome-home routine** — first two minutes after reunion are phone-down, full attention.
- **The daily map question** — ask "what's one thing you're looking forward to / dreading today?"
- **The stress-reducing ten** — ten minutes each day just listening to the other's outside-world stress, no fixing.
- **One spoken appreciation** — name one specific thing your partner did, out loud, daily.
- **The kept-promise note** — text or tell your partner the moment you follow through on something small you said you would.
- **Tech-free dinner** — phones away for one meal a day, even fifteen minutes.
- **Bedtime gratitude** — one sentence each, last thing before sleep: "today I was grateful for..."

**This week's prompt:** Circle the one you'll try first. Tell your partner which one, and why you picked it.

## 6.3 — Page 30: The Day 30 Recommitment Ceremony (fully written)

*This isn't legally binding and doesn't need an officiant — just the two of you, a quiet ten minutes, and (optionally) the letters you drafted on Day 24.*

**Opening (read together or one partner reads aloud):**
"Thirty days ago, we named something true: we hadn't fallen out of love. We'd drifted. Today we're choosing to stop drifting."

**Spoken commitments (each partner completes and reads their own):**
- "In the next season, I choose to notice ______."
- "In the next season, I choose to speak ______, even when it's hard."
- "In the next season, I choose to protect our ritual of ______."
- "I choose you again — not because the thirty days fixed everything, but because I want to keep choosing you."

**Optional faith blessing (invitation, not requirement):**
A short prayer of thanks and asking for continued grace in the marriage — leave space for the couple's own words or a pastor-recommended line rather than scripting devotion for them.

**Optional symbolic gesture:** exchange the letters from Day 24, light a candle, or simply hold hands for a full minute in silence.

**Beyond Day 30 (closing note, same page):**
"The reset ends here. The habit doesn't. Choose one thing from these thirty days — the check-in card, one script line, one ritual — and keep it going past today. You don't need all of it. You need the one thing you'll actually keep doing in month two."

## 8. The Four Bonus Documents

**Bonus 1 — Communication Repair Script (printable)**
Single page, print-and-post design. Same six lines as page 14, reformatted for a fridge or wallet card, plus a short legend showing Gottman's four repair-attempt types (verbal, physical, humorous, behavioral) so couples can invent their own lines too, not just use the scripted ones.

**Bonus 2 — Recommitment Letter Template**
Fill-in-the-blank structure, used starting Day 24 and exchanged Day 30:
- "What I've noticed about us this month..."
- "What I appreciate in you that I don't say enough..."
- "What I'm choosing, starting today..."
- "What I promise for the next season..."

**Bonus 3 — 30 Nights of Prayer for Your Marriage**
One short shared prayer per night, matched to that week's theme (Nights 1–7 → Notice, 8–14 → Speak, 15–21 → Trust, 22–30 → Reconnect), each paired with a scripture reference only (translation left to your choice). Sample nights:
- *Night 3 (Notice):* Short prayer for eyes to see one another clearly again. Reference: Psalm 139:23–24.
- *Night 10 (Speak):* Prayer for gentle, honest words. Reference: Ephesians 4:15.
- *Night 17 (Trust):* Prayer of gratitude for small faithfulness. Reference: Luke 16:10.
- *Night 30 (Reconnect):* Prayer of thanksgiving and renewed commitment. Reference: Ecclesiastes 4:9–12.
(Remaining 26 nights follow the same one-prayer-plus-one-reference pattern per week's theme.)

**Bonus 4 — Weekly Check-In Card**
One page, used four times across the program (Days 1, 7, 14, 21) plus referenced again Day 22. Three questions only:
1. How are we, really?
2. What do we need right now?
3. What's one small next step?
Designed to take five minutes and to double as the Day 1 Quick Win.
